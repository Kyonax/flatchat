With no mode flag the client is the app (S1.04, S1.09–S1.11): it signs in through the system browser, lists the core's approved worlds, joins one, and puts the player on the plaza with chat and proximity voice. The measuring modes (`--smoke`, `--bench`, `--crowd`, the voice tests, the lounge, the census) are untouched; `App.vue` picks `ClientApp` only when none is in the query.

## Signing In

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Only the main process talks to the core. The page sees `{ status, account, remembered }` and gets connect tokens, never an access or refresh token | The page renders other people's content |
| `shell.openExternal` only ever gets the core's own `/signin` page: `CoreApi.loginRequest` checks the origin (the trap linter's T16 is allowed on that one line, with the reason) | A compromised answer cannot send the browser elsewhere |
| The refresh token is encrypted with `safeStorage`; on Linux with the `basic_text` backend (no secret service) it is kept in memory only, and the home screen says the player will sign in again | `basic_text` is a fixed key: not protection |
| `Auth.access()` shares one refresh between concurrent callers | A refresh token works once; two refreshes at once read as a stolen token and the core ends the session |
| An unreachable core at start keeps the stored token; any other refusal deletes it | A flaky network must not sign people out |
| Signalling may go to the private addresses of Stage 0, or to the exact address the core gave with the latest connect token (`signal(url, offer, joinedAddress)`). When that address is the core's own relay (`CoreApi.isRelay`), the main process posts the offer there with the account's token | The page cannot steer signalling to an arbitrary host, and never sees the token |
| The join ticket carries the core's `iceServers` (cleaned: `stun:`/`turn:` URLs only), passed to the page's `RTCPeerConnection` | TURN reaches worlds behind carrier-grade NAT |
| The page's permissions: the microphone (audio only) and pointer lock, in the app and the lounge; nothing else | Electron grants everything unless told |

## Settings, Keys and Languages

- Bindings store `KeyboardEvent.code` values (layout-free: `KeyW` is where W is on a US keyboard) and `Mouse0`–`Mouse4`; at most two per action; one code belongs to one action (`bind` moves it); `menu` (Escape) is fixed. Labels come from `navigator.keyboard.getLayoutMap()` when Chromium has it, so a French player sees Z for `KeyW`.
- Every word on the app's screens goes through vue-i18n (`t('…')`). ESLint's `vue/no-bare-strings-in-template` fails any bare text in `src/renderer/src/ui/**/*.vue`, and a test checks `es.ts` has exactly `en.ts`'s keys, none empty. A new word means both files.
- vue-i18n's flags are defined in `electron.vite.config.ts` (composition API only; JIT message compilation, which uses no `eval`, so the page's policy keeps no `unsafe-eval`).
- The spike screens (`App.vue`'s HUD, the lounge, the crowd bench) still use global classes such as `.hud`; the app's views use their own (`.whud`). A global spike class once stretched the world's voice indicator from top to bottom.

## People, Roles and Coming Back (S3.06)

The roster's `role` feeds `WorldSession.state.people` (`world/people.ts`: `sortPeople`, this player first) and `state.role`. The People panel (`WorldView.vue`, action `people`, Tab) frees the pointer while open (`onLockChange` must not open the menu then) and shows a role select only where `canGiveTo(state.role, person)` allows, offering `rolesToGive(state.role)`; `session.giveRole` sends `role` and the roster answers. A `rule` or `banned` refusal shows the world's own words (`refusedDetail`, text only). A leave `closing` with detail `updating` (a world restarting on a new build) emits `rejoin`: `ClientApp.vue` waits until the core lists the world online, every 3 s for a minute, then joins it again; if it never comes back, the lost card offers the way out. Moderators see a Moderate button for people below them (`canModerate`; the form sends `moderate` with a reason). While the panel is open `session.typing` is true, so keys reach its fields instead of the avatar. A moderator's `muted` sets `state.heldMute` (a banner, and the microphone stops sending). A kick or ban shows `world.kicked` / `world.bannedHere` with the reason; a banned card (online worlds) shows `BanAppeal.vue` (`bridge.banAppeal`, `bridge.appealBan`, IPC `flatchat:ban-appeal` / `flatchat:appeal-ban`). Rule `roles-and-moderation`. Reports: the People panel's Report and the menu's Report this world open `ReportForm.vue`; `session.reportOf` gathers the evidence and the clip (only `world/report.ts` takes it, T20); the home screen lists the account's reports with their decisions. Esc opens the menu with the pointer free, and releases a held pointer otherwise. Rule `reports-and-safety`. Any overlay (menu, chat, People panel, report form) sets `session.typing` through one watcher, so the page's buttons and fields get their keys (Enter, Space) and the avatar does not. Mouse moves within 100 ms of a pointer lock, or larger than 400 px in one event, are ignored: Chromium's first move after a lock can swing the camera round.

Voice controls (S3.08): `chain.silenced` drops the voices of the accounts this player muted (`session.loadMutes`, `setPersonalMute`, the core's `/api/v1/me/mutes`, so mutes follow the account); `speakerLevel(d, range, worldGain, volume, earmuffs)` sets each speaker's level from its frames' `range` and `gain`, this player's volume for that person (`settings.voice.volumes`, by account) and earmuffs (action `earmuffs`, N: beyond 2.5 m a voice drops to 15 %).

Friends, blocks and minors (S3.10, S3.11): `session.loadSocial()` (online worlds) fetches the mutes and `bridge.social()` (friends, requests both ways, the blocked accounts, `under18` from `/api/v1/me`); a minor's microphone starts muted. `hidden` (the world's list) leaves those entities undrawn (`avatars`), unheard (`chain.silenced`) and unread (`mayRead` in the chat handler); for a minor, `mayRead` also drops everyone but its friends, so the client keeps the rule even with a world that does not. The People panel lists someone hidden only if this player blocked them (Unblock); Add friend / Accept / Asked and Block per person; `session.block` tells the core and sends `block` to the world at once. The home screen lists friends and the requests to answer. Rule `reports-and-safety`.

## The End-to-End Test

`node tools/bots/src/e2e.ts -- CLIENT [--shot FILE] [--shots DIR] [--keep]` runs Stage 1's path with every real program: a private PostgreSQL (`apps/core/scripts/db.ts`), the core, an account from an invitation, two worlds registered and approved through the admin command line, two world servers on plazas whose portals lead to each other (`tools/converter/scripts/make-travel.ts`; relayed through the core, downloaded by the client), and the packaged client under Playwright (`playwright-core`'s `_electron`). It replaces `shell.openExternal` in the main process, plays the browser over HTTP (the sign-in page, the form, the redirect to the loopback port), then clicks through the app: the plaza's row in the world browser, the microphone question, walking, a jump, a chat line, then walking backwards through the portal into the garden. It passes when the first world's summary shows 1 join, more than 10 applied poses, 1 chat line, at least 3 fetches, a `portal` and one `leave:travel`, and the second world 1 join. With `--relay` (S2.12) the client runs `--relay-only` (WebRTC's `iceTransportPolicy: 'relay'`, which Chromium enforces strictly) and the core hands out coturn credentials: the first join must fail while no relay runs (the world view's back button appears), then `startTurn` (`tools/bots/src/procs.ts`: coturn's `turnserver` on loopback, shared-secret credentials, `--allow-loopback-peers`, ready when it answers a STUN binding request) starts it and the same path goes through it. libjuice's relay policy (node-datachannel) does not stop direct checks, so this proof needs the real client, not the bots. It needs a display (`xvfb-run -a` in CI's Linux job) and PostgreSQL's programs. Give the client a fresh profile (`XDG_CONFIG_HOME`), or a remembered session skips the sign-in screen.

## Correct vs. Incorrect

**Incorrect:** handing the page a token to call the core itself.
```ts
contextBridge.exposeInMainWorld('flatchat', { accessToken: () => ipcRenderer.invoke('flatchat:token') });
```
**Correct:** the page asks for what it needs; the main process holds the session.
```ts
contextBridge.exposeInMainWorld('flatchat', { joinWorld: (id: string) => ipcRenderer.invoke('flatchat:join', id) });
```
**Why:** anything the page holds, a hostile world's content could read; a connect token is good for one world for 60 seconds, an account token for everything.

## Avatars in the World (S4.05 to S4.08)

`world/avatar-layer.ts` (`AvatarLayer`, created by the session when the bridge has `avatarFile`; offline worlds keep the placeholders): `wear(entity, manifest)` from each roster entry, `drop` when a player leaves, `update(states, camera, dt, now)` each frame answering the entities drawn as avatars (their capsule goes, `avatarDrawn`, but the name tag and speaking ring stay). Levels by distance and `policy` (`DEFAULT_POLICY`: 8 full within 8 m, 20 more at LOD1 within 18 m or LOD2 within 35 m, the rest impostors; this player's own always full; an avatar worse than `minRank` never full); a switch waits 700 ms; one fetch and parse at a time (`parseLevel` in `world/avatar-loader.ts`: `checkAvatarLevel` from packages/content first, three-vrm with MToon node materials, one skeleton, shared textures, no outline). Impostors of one avatar share one parsed template; their clones are posed with three-vrm's rule (raw = parentWorld⁻¹ · normalized · parentWorld · rest). Poses: `world/avatar-pose.ts` (pure, tested): `gaitOf` (idle, walk, run, air, sit), `locomotion`, `strideRate`, `handPose` (the seven `HAND_POSES`), `mouthShapes` from the voice frame. Expressions from the roster's face (keys 1 to 5 toggle happy, angry, sad, relaxed, surprised; R steps through the hand poses; `session.setFace` sends `face`), blinks every few seconds, spring bones (`vrm.update`) on the nearest `springs` (6). VRM faces +Z and a player's yaw 0 faces -Z: the holder turns by π. The view starts in third person: the end-to-end test's `1-joined` shot shows this player's own avatar from behind (`data-test=avatar-counts` carries the counts per level). `counts()` gives the avatars drawn per level; `status()` (`busy`, `entries`, `templates`, `failed`, `lastError`) is the first thing to read when avatars stay as placeholders: a load that never settles shows as `busy` with no templates and nothing failed (S4.12's KTX2 hang looked exactly like that). The layer culls: each avatar's meshes have `frustumCulled` off (skinned meshes move past their bounds), and three.js culls meshes, never groups, so `update` tests a sphere around each body (its height × 0.8, centred halfway up) against the camera's frustum, built as the renderer builds it (`coordinateSystem`, `reversedDepth`), and an avatar out of view is neither posed nor drawn (`inView` counts the rest; this player's own always is). Before S4.12 nothing was culled: all 200 of a crowd were posed and drawn every frame. What a frame costs is the render call's CPU side, about 30 µs per skinned impostor and 70 µs per MToon primitive with morph targets, whatever the material (a Lambert impostor measured the same): fewer objects is the lever, not simpler shading.

## The Viewer's Policy (S4.05 tier, S4.09)

`world/avatar-policy.ts` (pure, tested): `tierOf` (the first visit's frames from the third to the fifteenth second, p95: ≤ 12 ms strong, ≤ 22 ms medium, else light), `TIER_POLICY` (full / lower / springs / worst rank in full: light 4/12/2/good, medium 8/20/6/medium, strong 12/28/8/poor), `FrameTuner` (p90 over 2 s: past 33 ms the factor drops 0.15 down to 0.25, under 20 ms it climbs 0.1 back to 1), `viewerPolicy(settings.avatar, factor, viewer)` (the viewer's own always full; `hidden` accounts and strangers below `shield` that are not friends as impostors). The session refreshes the layer's policy four times a second (`tunePolicy`) and sets `state.measuredTier`, which `WorldView` writes into the settings with its tier's defaults.
